Methodology & Notes

Measurement set. The 16 rows are a tomographically complete set: each is a coincidence measurement between mode 1 projected onto one of {H, V, D, L, R} and mode 2 projected onto one of {H, V, D, L, R}, where |D>=(|H>+|V>)/sqrt2, |L>=(|H>+i|V>)/sqrt2, |R>=(|H>-i|V>)/sqrt2. The first four settings (HH, HV, VV, VH) must stay in that order — their sum fixes the overall pair-production rate N.

Linear tomography. Solves n_n = N<psi_n|rho|psi_n> for the 15 free real parameters of a Hermitian, unit-trace rho by least squares. This estimate is exact when the data are noise-free, but for real (noisy) counts it routinely has negative eigenvalues — i.e. it isn't a physical quantum state. This tool flags that explicitly.

Maximum likelihood. rho is written as rho = T†T / Tr(T†T), with T a lower-triangular matrix of 16 real parameters — by construction this rho is always Hermitian, unit-trace and positive-semidefinite. The parameters are fit by minimizing a Gaussian log-likelihood using gradient-based optimization (Adam, numerical gradients, multiple random restarts from a positivity-corrected linear estimate).

Validation. The linear-tomography step was checked against an independently worked reference calculation to 4 decimal places. The maximum-likelihood step converges to the same optimum from many independent starting points and under several different optimizers (Adam, BFGS, Powell), which is evidence it reaches the true global optimum rather than a local one.

Not included: propagation of Poissonian shot-noise into error bars on rho — that requires a full derivative or Monte-Carlo error analysis and is a natural extension if you need confidence intervals.
